APPSC deep core

  • Article 368 governs Parliament's constituent power and amendment procedure; some amendments additionally require ratification by at least half of the State legislatures.
  • Know three procedural categories used in study: provisions changed by simple majority outside Article 368; special majority; and special majority plus State ratification.
  • Kesavananda Bharati (1973): Parliament may amend the Constitution but cannot damage/destroy its basic structure.
  • The Court has identified basic features across cases rather than through one exhaustive constitutional list; examples commonly recognised include supremacy of the Constitution, republican/democratic government, secularism, federalism, judicial review and separation-of-powers-related principles.
  • Minerva Mills: important for limited amending power and harmony between Fundamental Rights and DPSPs.

Prelims traps

Article 368 • State-ratification matters • Kesavananda year • basic structure is judicial doctrine, not an enumerated constitutional schedule.

Mains framework

Need for amendment → procedure → Parliament's power → judicial review → Kesavananda → later development → flexibility vs constitutional identity.
