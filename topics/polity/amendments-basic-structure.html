Constitutional Amendments & Basic Structure

APPSC Group-I • Prelims + Mains integrated chapter

High-yield constitutional facts

  • Kesavananda Bharati (1973) held that Parliament's amending power does not extend to destroying the Constitution's basic structure.
  • Not every constitutional amendment requires ratification by States.
  • Judicial review and limited amending power are central to basic-structure jurisprudence.

How to study for Prelims

Revise Part/Article → institution/right/principle → exception or limitation → amendment/case link. Statement questions often mix a correct constitutional idea with an incorrect Article, beneficiary, procedure or legal effect.

Mains analytical approach

Start with the constitutional provision or principle, explain its purpose, trace important institutional/judicial development, identify tensions in implementation, and finish with constitutional balance. Avoid treating rights, institutions and principles as absolute where the Constitution itself provides qualifications.

Connections

  • Constitutionalism: limited government, rule of law and accountable power.
  • Democracy: representation plus rights, institutions and checks and balances.
  • Social transformation: equality and liberty operate alongside welfare-oriented constitutional commitments.
  • Judiciary: interpretation and judicial review shape the working meaning of many provisions.

Practice MCQs — not official PYQs

  1. Constitutional provisions should be revised together with their legal effect and important exceptions. True
  2. Every constitutional principle operates without qualifications or limitations. False
  3. Judicial interpretation can be relevant to understanding constitutional provisions. True
  4. An unverified question should be labelled an official APPSC PYQ. False

Mains practice

Explain the constitutional significance of Constitutional Amendments & Basic Structure. Discuss its evolution, practical operation and major issues in maintaining constitutional balance.

Answer structure: provision/history → purpose → key features → case/amendment where relevant → contemporary governance significance → balanced conclusion.
