Distribution of Legislative Powers

APPSC Group-I • Prelims + Mains integrated

Core coverage

Constitutional distribution of legislative competence between Union and States; Union/State/Concurrent Lists, residuary power, circumstances for Parliament to legislate on State subjects and federal dispute dimensions.

Prelims focus

Constitutional/legal basis • institutions • powers • procedures • accountability • Centre–State and Andhra relevance.

Rapid revision

Definition/context → 5 key points → 3 examples → 2 challenges → 2 reforms → one-line conclusion.

APPSC deep core

  • Article 246 + Seventh Schedule form the basic legislative-distribution framework: Union List, State List and Concurrent List.
  • Residuary power: Parliament has power over matters not enumerated in the State or Concurrent Lists, subject to the constitutional scheme.
  • Parliament can legislate on State List matters in constitutionally specified circumstances, including mechanisms under Articles 249, 250, 252 and 253.
  • Repugnancy: Article 254 addresses inconsistency between Parliamentary and State laws on Concurrent List matters, including the constitutional role of Presidential assent in specified cases.
  • Distinguish legislative competence from administrative implementation and fiscal assignment—they are related but separate federal questions.

Prelims traps

List–subject • residuary power • Article 249 vs 252 • emergency legislation • treaty implementation • Article 254 repugnancy.

Mains framework

Federal principle → three Lists → exceptional Union power → repugnancy → courts/interpretation → coordination → balance of national and regional interests.
