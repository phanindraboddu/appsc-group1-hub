🧭 1. Overview

The Vijayanagara Empire emerged in the fourteenth century and became one of the most powerful political formations of South India.

It played an important role in the political, economic and cultural history of the Telugu-speaking regions, particularly Rayalaseema and coastal Andhra.

For APPSC, the most important themes are the four dynasties, Krishnadevaraya, conflicts with the Gajapatis, Udayagiri and Kondavidu campaigns, the Battle of Talikota, Telugu literature, temple patronage and the later centres at Penukonda and Chandragiri.

⚡ 30-Second Overview

🏛️ Foundation

Vijayanagara was founded in the fourteenth century by Harihara and Bukka of the Sangama line.

👑 Greatest Ruler

Krishnadevaraya of the Tuluva dynasty.

⚔️ Andhra Focus

Udayagiri, Kondavidu, Kondapalli and Gajapati campaigns.

💥 Turning Point

Battle of Talikota / Rakshasi-Tangadi, 1565.

👑 2. Four Dynasties

1. Sangama

The founding dynasty of Vijayanagara. Harihara I and Bukka I belonged to this line.

2. Saluva

Saluva Narasimha established the Saluva line during a period of political instability.

3. Tuluva

The dynasty reached its greatest prominence under Krishnadevaraya.

4. Aravidu

The Aravidu dynasty ruled after Talikota, with political centres shifting south-eastward.

🎯 Memory: Sangama → Saluva → Tuluva → Aravidu

👑 3. Important Rulers

01

Harihara I

Harihara I was one of the founders of the Vijayanagara state and belonged to the Sangama dynasty.

🎯 Founder generation: Harihara I + Bukka I.

02

Bukka I

Bukka I consolidated and expanded the early Vijayanagara state.

🎯 Early consolidation of Vijayanagara.

03

Devaraya I

Devaraya I strengthened the kingdom and was involved in conflicts with neighbouring Deccan powers.

04

Devaraya II

Devaraya II was one of the strongest Sangama rulers and presided over an important phase of military and cultural development.

🔥 Important Sangama ruler.

05

Saluva Narasimha

Saluva Narasimha replaced the weakened Sangama line and established the Saluva dynasty.

06

🔥🔥🔥 Krishnadevaraya

Krishnadevaraya was the most celebrated ruler of the Tuluva dynasty and one of the most important rulers for APPSC examination preparation.

His reign witnessed major military campaigns, political expansion, economic prosperity, temple patronage and a remarkable flourishing of Telugu literature.

His campaigns against the Gajapati rulers brought important Telugu territories such as Udayagiri, Kondavidu and Kondapalli under Vijayanagara control.

🔥 Krishnadevaraya → Tuluva → Gajapati campaigns → Udayagiri → Kondavidu → Kondapalli → Telugu literature.

07

Achyutadevaraya

Achyutadevaraya succeeded Krishnadevaraya. His reign marks the beginning of increasing factional struggles within Vijayanagara politics.

08

Aliya Rama Raya

Rama Raya became the dominant political figure during the nominal reign of Sadasiva Raya.

He played an active role in the politics of the Deccan Sultanates before Vijayanagara suffered defeat at the Battle of Talikota in 1565.

🔥 Rama Raya → Sadasiva Raya → Talikota, 1565.

09

Tirumala Deva Raya

Tirumala played an important role in preserving the Vijayanagara political structure after the disaster of Talikota.

He became associated with the establishment of the Aravidu dynasty.

🎯 Aravidu dynasty → post-Talikota phase.

⚔️ 4. Krishnadevaraya's Andhra Campaigns

🔥 APPSC HIGH PRIORITY: This section is more important for Andhra Pradesh examinations than merely memorising all Vijayanagara wars.

⚔️ Udayagiri Campaign

Udayagiri was an important Gajapati stronghold in present-day Andhra Pradesh.

Krishnadevaraya's eastern campaign began with operations against Udayagiri.

⚔️ Kondavidu

After Udayagiri, Vijayanagara forces advanced further into the Andhra region.

Kondavidu was captured in 1515 after a major campaign against the Gajapati forces.

⚔️ Kondapalli

Vijayanagara subsequently captured Kondapalli, strengthening its position in the Krishna region.

⚔️ Coastal Andhra

Krishnadevaraya continued his advance through Telugu territories toward the northern Andhra coast.

⚔️ Gajapati Conflict

Krishnadevaraya fought Prataparudra Gajapati for control of important territories in coastal Andhra and adjoining regions.

🛕 Temple Visits

Inscriptions record Krishnadevaraya's visits and gifts to important religious centres including Amaravati, Srisailam and other temples.

🗺️ Andhra Campaign Sequence

🔥 Remember this flow

Udayagiri → Vinukonda / Bellamkonda region → Kondavidu → Vijayawada region → Kondapalli → further advance toward coastal Andhra

⚔️ 5. Battle of Raichur

Krishnadevaraya's conflict over the Raichur Doab was one of the most famous military episodes of his reign.

Vijayanagara fought the Bijapur Sultanate for control over this strategically important region between the Krishna and Tungabhadra rivers.

🎯 Don't confuse: Raichur → Krishnadevaraya

Talikota → Rama Raya

💥 6. Battle of Talikota / Rakshasi-Tangadi

1565 — Major Turning Point

The Battle of Talikota was fought in 1565 between Vijayanagara forces led by Aliya Rama Raya and an alliance of major Deccan Sultanates.

Vijayanagara was defeated and Rama Raya was killed.

The imperial capital was subsequently devastated, producing a major political crisis.

However, Vijayanagara political authority did not disappear immediately. The empire continued under the Aravidu dynasty from new centres.

🔥 APPSC Concept: Do not write that Vijayanagara Empire "completely ended in 1565." Talikota destroyed the dominance of the old capital and greatly weakened the empire, but the Aravidu rulers continued Vijayanagara political authority afterward.

🌾 7. Vijayanagara and Andhra Pradesh

🔥 Rayalaseema

Rayalaseema formed an important political and strategic zone within the Vijayanagara world.

🔥 Penukonda

Penukonda became an important centre of Vijayanagara authority after the fall of the imperial capital.

🔥 Chandragiri

Chandragiri later became an important Aravidu-period political centre.

🛕 Tirupati

Vijayanagara rulers were major patrons of the Tirumala-Tirupati temple complex.

🎨 Lepakshi

Lepakshi is an important example of Vijayanagara-period temple architecture, sculpture and painting in present-day Andhra Pradesh.

🏰 Kondavidu

Kondavidu was strategically important in Vijayanagara-Gajapati struggles for Andhra.

🏛️ 8. Administration

King

The monarch occupied the central position in administration.

Nayankara System

Military chiefs or Nayakas exercised authority over assigned territories and supplied troops to the state.

Amaram

Territorial assignments associated with Nayakas are commonly discussed under the Amaram system.

Local Administration

Local institutions continued to play a role in village and regional administration.

💰 9. Economy

🌾 Agriculture

Agriculture formed the economic foundation of the empire.

💧 Irrigation

Tanks, canals and irrigation works supported agricultural expansion.

🛍️ Markets

Urban centres contained active markets for agricultural and manufactured goods.

🚢 Trade

Vijayanagara participated in extensive inland and overseas trade networks.

🛕 10. Religion, Art and Architecture

Vijayanagara rulers patronised temples and religious institutions on a large scale.

Temple complexes became important religious, social, cultural and economic institutions.

Important features of Vijayanagara architecture include large temple complexes, tall gateway towers, pillared halls and elaborate sculpture.

🏛️ Hampi

Monumental centre of Vijayanagara architecture.

🎨 Lepakshi

Famous in Andhra Pradesh for temple architecture, sculpture and mural traditions.

🛕 Tirumala

Received important royal patronage from Vijayanagara rulers.

📚 11. Telugu Literature

The Vijayanagara period was a major phase in the development of Telugu literature.

Krishnadevaraya himself composed the Telugu work Amuktamalyada.

His court is traditionally associated with the celebrated group of Telugu poets known as the Ashtadiggajas.

👑 Krishnadevaraya

King, military leader and literary patron.

📖 Amuktamalyada

Celebrated Telugu literary work associated with Krishnadevaraya.

✍️ Ashtadiggajas

Traditional group of eight eminent Telugu poets associated with his court.
