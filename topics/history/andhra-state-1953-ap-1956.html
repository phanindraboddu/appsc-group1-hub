Actual study notes — Andhra State 1953 & Andhra Pradesh 1956

Roots of linguistic Andhra

The demand for a separate Andhra province developed during the colonial period through linguistic-cultural consciousness and political organisation. Telugu-speaking areas of the Madras Presidency sought a province where administration and public life could function more directly through Telugu.

Potti Sriramulu and 1953

Potti Sriramulu undertook a fast demanding a separate Andhra State and died in December 1952. The mass reaction accelerated the Union government's decision. Andhra State was formed on 1 October 1953 from Telugu-speaking districts of Madras State; Kurnool became the capital.

States Reorganisation Commission

The Government of India appointed the SRC in 1953 under Fazl Ali, with H.N. Kunzru and K.M. Panikkar as members. It examined linguistic and other considerations in state reorganisation.

Formation of Andhra Pradesh

On 1 November 1956, Andhra State merged with the Telugu-speaking Telangana region of the former Hyderabad State to create Andhra Pradesh, with Hyderabad as capital. The merger involved political discussion over safeguards and regional interests.

Why this matters

Andhra's experience became an important part of the wider history of linguistic state reorganisation and Indian federalism. For Mains, distinguish clearly between Andhra State (1953) and Andhra Pradesh (1956).
