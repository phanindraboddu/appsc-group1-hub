APPSC high-yield core

  • Kakatiyas: Orugallu, Ganapatideva, Rudramadevi and Prataparudra; nayankara, tanks, agrarian expansion and Motupalli trade.
  • Reddis: Kondavidu and Rajamahendravaram were important centres; study political competition together with Telugu literary patronage.
  • Gajapatis: Odisha-based power extended into parts of coastal Andhra, producing sustained competition for strategic forts and fertile regions.
  • Vijayanagara: Andhra territories were central to conflicts with Gajapatis and Deccan powers; Krishnadevaraya's Udayagiri and Kondavidu campaigns are high-yield.
  • Do not study these dynasties as separate lists: APPSC often rewards understanding of succession, conflict, forts, irrigation, trade and patronage.

Actual study notes — Kakatiyas, Reddis, Gajapatis & Vijayanagara

Kakatiya legacy

The Kakatiyas unified substantial Telugu-speaking territory around Warangal. Their collapse under Delhi Sultanate pressure in the early fourteenth century created a fragmented political environment in which new regional powers emerged.

Post-Kakatiya resistance and regional states

Local warrior elites and chiefs asserted authority after the fall of Warangal. The Musunuri chiefs are associated with attempts to restore regional control. Reddi kingdoms developed at Kondavidu and Rajahmundry, while other local powers competed across coastal Andhra and Rayalaseema.

Reddis

Prolaya Vema Reddi is associated with the foundation of Kondavidu Reddi power. Agriculture, forts, temples and literary patronage supported the state. Srinatha became an iconic Telugu literary figure of the wider Reddi-era cultural world.

Gajapatis

The Gajapati rulers of Odisha expanded southward and contested coastal Andhra. Their rivalry with Vijayanagara made forts and territories such as Udayagiri and Kondavidu strategically important.

Vijayanagara

Vijayanagara increasingly intervened in Andhra. Under Krishnadevaraya, campaigns against Gajapati power brought important Andhra territories under imperial authority. The period also represents a high point of Telugu literary patronage.

Political pattern

Rather than memorising dynasties separately, understand medieval Andhra as a contested zone connecting the eastern coast, Rayalaseema and Deccan interior. Forts, irrigation, agrarian revenue, ports and military chiefs were recurring sources of power.
