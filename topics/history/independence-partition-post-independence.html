Independence, Partition & Post-Independence

APPSC Group-I • Prelims + Mains integrated

Core coverage

Final phase of freedom struggle; constitutional negotiations, independence and Partition; integration of princely states, reorganisation and major challenges of post-independence consolidation.

Prelims focus

Chronology • personalities • places • organisations • acts/events • Andhra links.

Mains framework

Context → key features → institutional/social/economic dimensions → issues → evidence/examples → reforms/way forward → conclusion.

Practice

Prepare five factual cards and one 250-word analytical answer on Independence, Partition & Post-Independence. Practice only; official PYQ status requires verification from the APPSC paper.

Rapid revision

Definition/context → 5 key points → 3 examples → 2 challenges → 2 reforms → one-line conclusion.

Exam-ready core

  • The transfer of power in 1947 must be studied through late-colonial constitutional negotiations, mass politics, communal conflict and the breakdown of efforts to agree on a shared constitutional arrangement.
  • Partition: produced immense displacement, violence and refugee rehabilitation challenges; avoid monocausal explanations.
  • Integration: the new Union addressed the accession and integration of princely states through political, constitutional and administrative processes.
  • Constitution: the Constituent Assembly completed a republican constitutional framework that came into force on 26 January 1950.
  • Reorganisation: linguistic and regional demands led to subsequent state reorganisation, with Andhra State (1953) an especially important APPSC case.

Prelims focus

Cabinet Mission • Mountbatten Plan • Indian Independence Act • accession • Constitution dates • Andhra State/SRC chronology.

Mains framework

End of colonial rule → Partition → rehabilitation → princely-state integration → Constitution → democratic consolidation → linguistic reorganisation.
