Exam-ready core

  • The transfer of power in 1947 must be studied through late-colonial constitutional negotiations, mass politics, communal conflict and the breakdown of efforts to agree on a shared constitutional arrangement.
  • Partition: produced immense displacement, violence and refugee rehabilitation challenges; avoid monocausal explanations.
  • Integration: the new Union addressed the accession and integration of princely states through political, constitutional and administrative processes.
  • Constitution: the Constituent Assembly completed a republican constitutional framework that came into force on 26 January 1950.
  • Reorganisation: linguistic and regional demands led to subsequent state reorganisation, with Andhra State (1953) an especially important APPSC case.

Actual study notes — Independence, Partition & Post-Independence India

End of colonial rule

The final phase of British rule was shaped by World War II, Quit India, the INA episode and trials, post-war labour and military unrest, elections and negotiations among the British government, Congress, Muslim League and other political actors.

Cabinet Mission and Partition

The Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946 attempted to preserve an Indian union with a complex federal arrangement, but political agreement did not endure. Communal violence intensified. The Mountbatten Plan accepted partition, and the Indian Independence Act 1947 created the two dominions of India and Pakistan.

Human consequences

Partition caused enormous displacement, communal violence and refugee crises. Mains answers should include this social and humanitarian dimension rather than treating Partition only as constitutional negotiation.

Integration and Constitution

The new Indian government faced integration of princely states and institution-building. The Constituent Assembly completed the Constitution, adopted on 26 November 1949 and brought into force on 26 January 1950.

Democratic consolidation

Universal adult franchise, the first general elections of 1951–52, parliamentary government and federal institutions established a democratic framework on an exceptional scale.

State reorganisation

Linguistic demands produced major territorial changes. Andhra State was formed in 1953; the States Reorganisation Act reshaped the map in 1956 and Andhra Pradesh was formed on 1 November 1956.

Economic development

Post-independence development used planning, public-sector investment, agricultural programmes and institution-building. Outcomes and priorities changed across decades; avoid presenting the entire post-1947 economy as one unchanging model.
