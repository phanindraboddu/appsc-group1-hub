Magadha & Mauryas

APPSC Group-I • Ancient India + State Formation • Reading-ready chapter

1. Magadha before the Mauryas

Haryanka, Shishunaga and Nanda phases prepared the political setting for Mauryan expansion. Bimbisara and Ajatashatru are important for early Magadhan consolidation.

2. Chandragupta Maurya

Chandragupta founded the Mauryan Empire after the Nanda period. The empire expanded across a large part of the subcontinent, while its north-western frontier interacted with the Hellenistic world.

3. Administration

The Mauryan state combined central authority with provincial and local administration. Revenue, officials, military organisation, roads and communication were essential to imperial control.

4. Sources

Ashokan inscriptions are primary evidence for royal policy. Arthashastra is important but should be used carefully because its textual compilation history is complex. Greek accounts and archaeology supplement the evidence.

5. Ashoka

After the Kalinga war, Ashoka's inscriptions emphasised dhamma, ethical conduct, tolerance, welfare and communication with subjects. Dhamma should not be reduced to a simple synonym for Buddhism.

6. Economy and society

Agriculture and taxation supported the state; trade routes and urban centres connected regions. Mauryan integration varied across the empire.

7. Decline

After Ashoka, imperial cohesion weakened and regional powers emerged. Avoid attributing the decline to one simplistic cause.

8. Prelims + Mains

Revise rulers, sources, inscriptions, dhamma, administration and Kalinga together. Mains structure: pre-Mauryan context → expansion → administration → Ashoka → economy → decline → historical significance.
