Magadha & Mauryas

APPSC Group-I • Detailed Prelims + Mains chapter

Core syllabus

  • Haryankas, Shishunagas and Nandas
  • Bimbisara and Ajatashatru
  • Chandragupta Maurya and Mauryan expansion
  • Ashoka: dhamma and inscriptions
  • Mauryan administration and economy
  • Arthashastra and Megasthenes as sources
  • Ashokan edicts and script/geography
  • Mauryan decline and legacy

High-yield facts

  • Chandragupta Maurya established the Mauryan Empire after the Nanda period.
  • Ashoka's inscriptions are primary evidence for his dhamma policy and administration.
  • Major rock and pillar edicts occur across a wide geographical area; decipherment of Brahmi was crucial to modern understanding.
  • Megasthenes' Indica survives in fragments quoted by later authors.
  • Arthashastra is an important political-economic text, but its date and relationship to Mauryan practice require careful handling.

Historical analysis

Read this topic through state formation → administration → economy → society → religion/culture → regional interaction → change over time. In Mains, avoid a ruler-by-ruler narrative unless the question asks for chronology.

Prelims traps

  • Match personalities with the correct dynasty and period.
  • Distinguish an administrative institution from a modern equivalent.
  • Do not turn a literary tradition into direct administrative evidence without qualification.
  • Separate political control from cultural influence.

Practice MCQs — not official PYQs

  1. Historical institutions should be interpreted in their period context. True
  2. A single ruler is always sufficient to explain the rise or fall of a dynasty. False
  3. Inscriptions, coins, architecture and literature can complement one another as historical sources. True
  4. These questions are official PYQs. False

Mains practice

Examine the political, economic and cultural significance of Magadha & Mauryas. Use chronology only where it helps establish change and causation.

Structure: context → political development → administration/economy → society/culture → regional impact → conclusion.
