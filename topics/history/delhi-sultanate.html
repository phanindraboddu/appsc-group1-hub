Full chapter notes

Background and dynasties

The Delhi Sultanate developed after the Ghurid conquests and lasted from the early thirteenth to early sixteenth century. For APPSC, remember the dynastic order: Mamluk/Slave → Khalji → Tughlaq → Sayyid → Lodi.

Mamluks

  • Qutb al-Din Aibak: established the first ruling house at Delhi after Muhammad Ghori; associated with the beginning of Qutb Minar.
  • Iltutmish: consolidated the Sultanate, strengthened Delhi and completed major early institutional consolidation.
  • Razia: notable woman ruler of the Sultanate.
  • Balban: emphasised strong monarchy and suppression of powerful nobles/rebellions.

Khaljis

Alauddin Khalji pursued territorial expansion, military reforms and market/price-control measures designed especially to support a large army. His general Malik Kafur led campaigns into the Deccan and south India.

Tughlaqs

  • Muhammad bin Tughlaq: remembered for ambitious experiments including the transfer of the capital toward Daulatabad and token currency; analyse objectives separately from implementation problems.
  • Firoz Shah Tughlaq: associated with public works, canals and construction as well as a different administrative approach.

Sayyids and Lodis

The Sayyids were followed by the Afghan Lodi dynasty. Ibrahim Lodi was defeated by Babur at the First Battle of Panipat (1526), conventionally marking the end of the Delhi Sultanate and beginning of Mughal rule.

Administration

  • The iqta system assigned revenue from territories to officials in return for administrative/military obligations; it was not identical to private ownership of land.
  • Important offices and departments varied over time; study Sultan, wazir/diwan, military organisation, revenue administration and provincial authority as an evolving system.
  • The relationship between central power, nobles, military commanders and local intermediaries was central to Sultanate politics.

Economy and society

Agriculture remained fundamental, while towns, crafts, monetisation and long-distance trade were significant. New political elites interacted with existing social structures; cultural exchange and conflict both formed part of the period.

Architecture and culture

Sultanate architecture combined arches, domes, minarets, calligraphy and local building traditions. Qutb complex, Alai Darwaza and Tughlaq monuments are high-yield examples. Persian became highly important in court and administration, while regional languages and composite cultural traditions also developed.

Andhra/Deccan linkage

Delhi's expansion under the Khaljis and Tughlaqs directly affected Deccan kingdoms. Malik Kafur's campaigns reached the Kakatiya kingdom; later Tughlaq expansion contributed to major political changes in the Deccan, an essential bridge to Kakatiya decline, Vijayanagara and Bahmani history.
