Delhi Sultanate

APPSC Group-I • Detailed Prelims + Mains chapter

Core syllabus

  • Mamluk/Slave, Khalji, Tughlaq, Sayyid and Lodi dynasties
  • Iqta system and administration
  • Alauddin Khalji's market and revenue measures
  • Muhammad bin Tughlaq's experiments
  • Firoz Shah Tughlaq and public works
  • Architecture, Persian culture and Sufi networks
  • Regional kingdoms and Sultanate limits

High-yield facts

  • The Delhi Sultanate is conventionally divided into five dynasties.
  • Alauddin Khalji introduced major market and military-revenue measures.
  • Muhammad bin Tughlaq is associated with the token currency experiment and capital transfer to Daulatabad.
  • Iqta was a system of revenue assignment linked with administration and military obligations; it should not simply be equated with private land ownership.
  • Sultanate rule interacted with powerful regional states rather than exercising uniform control over all of India.

Historical analysis

Read this topic through state formation → administration → economy → society → religion/culture → regional interaction → change over time. In Mains, avoid a ruler-by-ruler narrative unless the question asks for chronology.

Prelims traps

  • Match personalities with the correct dynasty and period.
  • Distinguish an administrative institution from a modern equivalent.
  • Do not turn a literary tradition into direct administrative evidence without qualification.
  • Separate political control from cultural influence.

Practice MCQs — not official PYQs

  1. Historical institutions should be interpreted in their period context. True
  2. A single ruler is always sufficient to explain the rise or fall of a dynasty. False
  3. Inscriptions, coins, architecture and literature can complement one another as historical sources. True
  4. These questions are official PYQs. False

Mains practice

Examine the political, economic and cultural significance of Delhi Sultanate. Use chronology only where it helps establish change and causation.

Structure: context → political development → administration/economy → society/culture → regional impact → conclusion.
