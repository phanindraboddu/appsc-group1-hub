Actual study notes — Indian Freedom Movement

Early nationalism

The Indian National Congress was founded in 1885. Early nationalists used petitions, resolutions and constitutional agitation while developing an all-India critique of colonial economic and administrative policies.

Swadeshi phase

The 1905 partition of Bengal triggered the Swadeshi and boycott movements. Nationalist politics expanded through indigenous enterprise, national education and mass mobilisation. The Surat split of 1907 reflected differences within Congress.

Home Rule and Lucknow

Bal Gangadhar Tilak and Annie Besant led Home Rule Leagues in 1916. The Lucknow Pact of 1916 marked Congress–Muslim League cooperation at that stage.

Gandhian mass phase

  • Champaran (1917), Ahmedabad (1918), Kheda (1918) established Gandhi's methods in Indian politics.
  • Non-Cooperation (1920–22) mobilised broad sections; Gandhi suspended it after Chauri Chaura.
  • Civil Disobedience: Salt March/Dandi in 1930; Gandhi-Irwin Pact and Round Table context followed.
  • Quit India (1942): launched amid World War II with the demand for British withdrawal; leaders were rapidly arrested but resistance spread in varied forms.

Other strands

Revolutionary nationalism, peasant and worker mobilisation, socialist currents, constitutional politics, Subhas Bose and the INA, and struggles in princely states formed additional strands. Freedom was not the product of one organisation or tactic alone.

Constitutional milestones

1909, 1919 and 1935 reforms altered colonial constitutional structures without satisfying nationalist demands. The Government of India Act 1935 introduced provincial autonomy and became an important institutional background to later constitutional development.

Andhra participation

Andhra witnessed strong nationalist mobilisation, including the Chirala-Perala movement, forest/tribal resistance associated with Alluri Sitarama Raju, Salt Satyagraha activity and constructive/nationalist work by numerous Telugu leaders.
