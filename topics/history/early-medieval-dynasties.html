Exam-ready core

  • Political pattern: post-Gupta India saw multiple regional states and changing centres of power rather than a single continuous empire.
  • Harsha: revise political career, Xuanzang's account and Banabhatta as sources, while separating courtly description from independently verifiable evidence.
  • Tripartite struggle: Gurjara-Pratiharas, Palas and Rashtrakutas competed for influence over Kanauj.
  • Deccan/South: Chalukyas, Pallavas, Rashtrakutas and Cholas are central for APPSC; know capitals, rulers, conflicts and cultural centres.
  • Agrarian change: land grants, new settlements, temple institutions and local intermediaries are major themes in interpretations of early medieval society.
  • Temples: architecture, patronage and temple-centred economic/social networks should be studied together.

Actual study notes — Early Medieval Dynasties

Meaning of early medieval

The period roughly between the post-Gupta centuries and the establishment of major Sultanate power saw strong regional kingdoms, agrarian expansion, land grants, temple-centred institutions and changing networks of political authority. Historians debate the extent to which these changes should be described as “feudal”.

Harsha and north India

Harshavardhana ruled from the early seventh century and made Kanauj a major political centre. Banabhatta's Harshacharita, Xuanzang's account and inscriptions are important sources. Pulakeshin II checked Harsha's southern advance.

Chalukyas and Pallavas

Badami Chalukyas and Pallavas competed across the Deccan and Tamil region. Pulakeshin II and Narasimhavarman I are central figures. Aihole, Badami, Pattadakal and Mamallapuram are major architectural centres.

Tripartite struggle

The Gurjara-Pratiharas, Palas and Rashtrakutas competed for influence over Kanauj. Dharmapala and Devapala are major Pala rulers; Nagabhata II and Mihira Bhoja are major Pratiharas; Dhruva and Govinda III represent Rashtrakuta intervention in north India.

Southern regional powers

Rashtrakutas, Cholas, Eastern and Western Chalukyas, Hoysalas and other dynasties developed powerful regional states. Their politics connected warfare with agrarian resources, temples, trade and local elites.

Andhra

The Eastern Chalukyas of Vengi are especially important: Kubja Vishnuvardhana founded the line, while Rajaraja Narendra's age is associated with Nannaya and the emergence of major Telugu literary production. Vengi also became a key arena in Chola–Western Chalukya politics.
