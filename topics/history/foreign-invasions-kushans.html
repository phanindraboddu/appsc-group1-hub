Revision rule

Remember 5 facts + 3 sources/places + 2 contributions + 1 analytical conclusion.

Exam-ready core

  • After the Mauryas, north-western India saw Indo-Greek, Shaka, Parthian and Kushan political formations; learn their sequence without treating each as a simple one-time invasion.
  • Indo-Greeks: Menander/Milinda is associated with the Buddhist dialogue Milindapanho; bilingual coinage is important evidence.
  • Shakas: Rudradaman I and the Junagadh inscription are high-yield; the inscription is notable for its elaborate Sanskrit prose.
  • Kushans: Kanishka is the best-known ruler; Kushan power connected north India with Central Asian trade networks.
  • Gandhara and Mathura developed as major artistic centres; compare material, iconography and artistic conventions rather than memorising a simplistic “foreign vs Indian” contrast.
  • Kushan coinage reflects extensive commerce and a diverse religious-cultural environment.

Prelims focus

Ruler–dynasty • inscription/text • coinage • Gandhara vs Mathura • trade routes • chronology.

Mains framework

Political change → trade/coinage → cultural interaction → Buddhism → art → long-distance connectivity → legacy.
