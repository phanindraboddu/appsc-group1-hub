Vedic Age & Mahajanapadas

APPSC Group-I • Ancient India • Reading-ready chapter

1. Early and Later Vedic phases

Early Vedic society was centred largely in the north-west; the Later Vedic phase saw expansion toward the Ganga-Yamuna region, wider agriculture and more complex political institutions. Read the change as a process, not a sudden break.

2. Economy and society

Pastoral activity remained important, while agriculture expanded in the Later Vedic period. Social differentiation became more pronounced and varna categories were articulated more clearly in later texts.

3. Political institutions

Sabha and Samiti are important Early Vedic institutions. Later political authority became more centralised around kingship, territorial control and revenue/tribute mechanisms.

4. Religion and literature

The Rigveda is the key Early Vedic source. Later Vedic texts include the Samaveda, Yajurveda, Atharvaveda, Brahmanas, Aranyakas and Upanishads. Sacrificial ritual became increasingly elaborate, while philosophical questioning also developed.

5. Mahajanapadas

By the sixth century BCE, larger territorial states and republic-like gana-sanghas existed. The sixteen Mahajanapadas are known from Buddhist and Jain traditions, with Magadha eventually becoming the dominant power.

6. Why Magadha rose

Favourable location, access to fertile areas and resources, river routes, and capable rulers helped Magadha expand. Avoid reducing its rise to one single factor.

7. Prelims traps

Early Vedic ≠ Later Vedic; Sabha/Samiti should not be treated as modern democratic institutions; Mahajanapadas belong to the later first-millennium-BCE transition; Vedic literature developed over a long period.

8. Mains framework

Explain the transition through geography → agriculture → settlement → social differentiation → political consolidation → Mahajanapadas → second urbanisation.
