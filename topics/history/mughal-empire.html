Mughal Empire

APPSC Group-I • Medieval India • Reading-ready chapter

1. Foundation and expansion

Babur defeated Ibrahim Lodi at Panipat in 1526. Humayun faced political reverses before restoration. Akbar established durable imperial structures and expanded Mughal authority.

2. Akbar's state

Mansabdari linked rank, service and military obligations. Jagirdars were assigned revenue rights rather than simply given ownership of land. The empire depended on relationships with regional elites.

3. Revenue

Todar Mal is associated with revenue measurement and assessment reforms under Akbar. Mughal revenue administration varied by region and period.

4. Jahangir and Shah Jahan

Imperial administration and culture continued, while architecture and court culture expanded. Shah Jahan's reign is particularly associated with monumental architecture.

5. Aurangzeb

Aurangzeb expanded Mughal territory extensively, especially in the Deccan, but prolonged warfare placed pressure on imperial resources and political relationships.

6. Economy and society

Agriculture was the fiscal base. Commercial networks, towns, artisans and merchants were important. Mughal India was integrated into wider Indian Ocean and overland trade.

7. Art and culture

Mughal painting, architecture, garden design, manuscript culture and Persian literary traditions are high-yield areas.

8. Decline and regionalisation

After Aurangzeb, succession conflicts, fiscal and military pressures, and regional assertion weakened central authority. Marathas, Sikhs, Jats and other regional powers became increasingly important.

9. AP angle

Deccan campaigns, Golconda and the Mughal incorporation of the Deccan connect Mughal history directly with Andhra/Deccan history.

10. Mains

Explain Mughal power through military expansion + mansab/jagir + revenue + elite integration + economy + culture, followed by the causes of eighteenth-century regionalisation.
