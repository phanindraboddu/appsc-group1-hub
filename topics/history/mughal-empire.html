Mughal Empire

APPSC Group-I • Detailed Prelims + Mains chapter

Core syllabus

  • Babur and First Battle of Panipat
  • Humayun and Sur interlude
  • Akbar: expansion and administrative consolidation
  • Mansabdari and jagirdari
  • Revenue system and Todar Mal
  • Jahangir, Shah Jahan and Aurangzeb
  • Art, architecture and literature
  • Regionalisation and decline

High-yield facts

  • Babur defeated Ibrahim Lodi at Panipat in 1526.
  • Akbar's administration used mansabdari ranking and jagir assignments within a broader imperial structure.
  • The zabt/revenue system is associated with measurement and assessment in territories where it was applied; it was not identical everywhere.
  • Shah Jahan's reign is associated with major imperial architecture including the Taj Mahal.
  • Aurangzeb's reign saw territorial expansion but also increasing fiscal and political pressures; Mughal decline had multiple causes.

Historical analysis

Read this topic through state formation → administration → economy → society → religion/culture → regional interaction → change over time. In Mains, avoid a ruler-by-ruler narrative unless the question asks for chronology.

Prelims traps

  • Match personalities with the correct dynasty and period.
  • Distinguish an administrative institution from a modern equivalent.
  • Do not turn a literary tradition into direct administrative evidence without qualification.
  • Separate political control from cultural influence.

Practice MCQs — not official PYQs

  1. Historical institutions should be interpreted in their period context. True
  2. A single ruler is always sufficient to explain the rise or fall of a dynasty. False
  3. Inscriptions, coins, architecture and literature can complement one another as historical sources. True
  4. These questions are official PYQs. False

Mains practice

Examine the political, economic and cultural significance of Mughal Empire. Use chronology only where it helps establish change and causation.

Structure: context → political development → administration/economy → society/culture → regional impact → conclusion.
