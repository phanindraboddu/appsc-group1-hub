Eastern Chalukyas

APPSC Group-I • Prelims + Mains integrated

Core coverage

Vengi, Kubja Vishnuvardhana, Chola relations, administration, religion and growth of Telugu literary culture including Nannaya.

Prelims checklist

Chronology • personalities/dynasties • places • texts/inscriptions • administration • economy • religion • art/architecture • source-based distinctions.

Mains framework

Context → political developments → administration → society/economy → religion/culture → art/literature → historical significance → Andhra/Deccan linkage where relevant.

Practice MCQs

  1. For this topic, chronology and primary sources should be studied together. Answer: True.
  2. Historical interpretation should be distinguished from direct textual/archaeological evidence. Answer: True.
  3. Regional linkages are useful in APPSC answers when relevant. Answer: True.

Practice only — not PYQs.

Mains practice

Discuss the political, socio-economic and cultural significance of Eastern Chalukyas.

Revision rule

Remember 5 facts + 3 sources/places + 2 contributions + 1 analytical conclusion.

APPSC high-yield core

  • Vengi: the Eastern Chalukyas became a major ruling line in the Andhra region and are central to AP-specific medieval history.
  • Origins: their establishment is connected with the expansion of the Badami Chalukyas into the eastern Deccan.
  • Political setting: succession struggles and relations with Rashtrakutas and Cholas repeatedly influenced Vengi politics.
  • Chola connection: dynastic interaction with the Cholas eventually produced a close Chola–Vengi political relationship.
  • Telugu: the period is crucial for the growth of Telugu literary culture; Rajaraja Narendra and Nannaya are especially important for APPSC.
  • Religion/culture: temple patronage, Brahmanical institutions and regional cultural integration expanded during the period.

Prelims focus

Prepare Vengi geography, founder/rulers, Chola links, Rajaraja Narendra–Nannaya association and the beginnings of the Telugu Mahabharatam tradition.

Mains angle

Political history alone is insufficient: connect Eastern Chalukya rule with the consolidation of regional culture and the development of Telugu language and literature.
