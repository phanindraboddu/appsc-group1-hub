Kanauj and Early Medieval India

APPSC Group-I • Prelims + Mains integrated

Core coverage

Harsha, Xuanzang, regionalisation, tripartite struggle, agrarian expansion, land grants, temples and regional cultures.

Prelims checklist

Chronology • personalities/dynasties • places • texts/inscriptions • administration • economy • religion • art/architecture • source-based distinctions.

Mains framework

Context → political developments → administration → society/economy → religion/culture → art/literature → historical significance → Andhra/Deccan linkage where relevant.

Practice only — not PYQs.

Mains practice

Discuss the political, socio-economic and cultural significance of Kanauj and Early Medieval India.

Revision rule

Remember 5 facts + 3 sources/places + 2 contributions + 1 analytical conclusion.

Exam-ready core

  • Harsha: ruled from the early seventh century; Banabhatta's Harshacharita and Xuanzang's account are important but must be read critically as different kinds of sources.
  • Kanauj: its strategic and symbolic importance contributed to later competition among Gurjara-Pratiharas, Palas and Rashtrakutas—the tripartite struggle.
  • Early medieval India saw proliferation of regional kingdoms, land grants, agrarian expansion, temple-centred institutions and changing networks of political authority.
  • Debates over “feudalism,” urban change and decentralisation are interpretations; in Mains, present evidence and regional variation rather than a single uniform model.

Prelims focus

Harsha–Banabhatta • Xuanzang • Kanauj • Pratihara–Pala–Rashtrakuta triangle • land-grant terminology.

Mains framework

Post-Gupta transition → Harsha → regionalisation → Kanauj contest → agrarian expansion → temples/land grants → trade/urban change → regional cultures.
