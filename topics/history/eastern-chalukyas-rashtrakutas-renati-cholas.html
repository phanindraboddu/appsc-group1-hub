Actual study notes — Eastern Chalukyas, Rashtrakutas & Renati Cholas

Eastern Chalukyas of Vengi

Kubja Vishnuvardhana, brother of Pulakeshin II, founded the eastern Chalukya line in the seventh century. Vengi's Krishna–Godavari setting gave the kingdom agricultural and strategic importance. Its politics repeatedly intersected with Rashtrakuta, Western Chalukya and Chola power.

Rashtrakuta interaction

The Rashtrakutas replaced the Badami Chalukyas as the dominant western Deccan power in the eighth century. Their interventions in Vengi succession disputes made Andhra an important arena of Deccan competition. Dantidurga founded Rashtrakuta imperial power; Krishna I is associated with the Kailasa temple at Ellora; Dhruva and Govinda III intervened in north Indian politics.

Rajaraja Narendra and Chola connection

Later Eastern Chalukya politics became deeply intertwined with the Cholas through warfare and marriage alliances. Rajaraja Narendra is especially important in Telugu cultural history because Nannaya began the Telugu Mahabharata under his patronage.

Renati Cholas

The Renati Cholas ruled parts of the Rayalaseema region. Their special importance for APPSC lies in epigraphy: their inscriptions are among the earliest substantial Telugu inscriptions, showing the growing use of Telugu in public records.

Historical significance

This period connects political regionalisation with agrarian expansion, temple patronage and the emergence of Telugu as an increasingly important inscriptional and literary language.
