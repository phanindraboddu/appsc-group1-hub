Exam-ready core

  • Sources: inscriptions, coins, Puranic references and archaeological remains are central to reconstructing Satavahana history.
  • Political geography: the dynasty linked the Deccan with western and eastern coastal trade zones; its history is especially important for Andhra/Deccan questions.
  • Gautamiputra Satakarni: a major ruler associated with restoration of Satavahana power; revise the Nasik inscription of Gautami Balashri and conflicts with western Kshatrapas.
  • Administration: monarchy with territorial/administrative units and local officials; inscriptions also illuminate grants and social groups.
  • Economy: agriculture, craft production, inland routes and long-distance trade; coins and Buddhist sites help trace commercial networks.
  • Religion & culture: Brahmanical patronage coexisted with support to Buddhist establishments; Prakrit was important in inscriptions/literary culture.
  • Art: Amaravati and other Buddhist centres are high-yield for Andhra-focused art, architecture and patronage questions.

Prelims traps

Do not treat Satavahana chronology as completely uncontested. Distinguish dynasty-wide features from evidence tied to a particular ruler or inscription. Revise ruler–inscription–site–coin pairs.

Mains dimensions

Actual study notes — Satavahanas

Origins and political history

The Satavahanas were a major Deccan power after the Mauryan age. Their chronology is reconstructed from inscriptions, coins, Puranic traditions and archaeology and contains scholarly debates. Simuka is generally treated as an early founder; Gautamiputra Satakarni and Vasisthiputra Pulumavi are major rulers.

Gautamiputra Satakarni

The Nasik inscription of Gautami Balashri is a key source. Gautamiputra fought the Western Kshatrapas and is associated with restoration/expansion of Satavahana power.

Administration

The kingdom used territorial units and officials while local chiefs and elites retained importance. Matronymic names such as Gautamiputra are a famous feature of royal nomenclature but should not automatically be interpreted as proof of a matriarchal political system.

Economy and trade

  • Agriculture, craft production, guilds and inland/coastal trade were important.
  • Coins and Roman trade evidence illustrate Deccan participation in wider commercial networks.
  • Ports and routes linked the Deccan with both western and eastern coasts.

Religion, society and language

Satavahana patronage operated in a plural religious environment involving Brahmanical traditions and Buddhism. Prakrit was important in inscriptions and literature. The Gatha Saptashati is traditionally associated with King Hala.

Art and Andhra

The Satavahana age is inseparable from the growth of Buddhist centres in the Andhra region. Amaravati became a major stupa and sculptural centre; other eastern Deccan Buddhist sites demonstrate strong mercantile and monastic networks. Avoid assigning every Amaravati phase to a single ruler.

Simuka–early founder • Gautamiputra–major ruler • Gautami Balashri–Nasik inscription • Pulumavi–successor • Hala–Gatha Saptashati • Amaravati–Buddhist art • Prakrit–important language.
