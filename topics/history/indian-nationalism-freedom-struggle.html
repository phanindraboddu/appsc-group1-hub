Actual study notes — Indian Nationalism & Freedom Struggle

Origins of nationalism

Modern Indian nationalism developed through colonial economic change, new education and print, political associations, improved communications and an increasingly shared critique of colonial rule. Early nationalists exposed the economic consequences of imperialism, including the drain of wealth.

Moderate phase

The Indian National Congress, founded in 1885, initially relied on petitions, resolutions, public opinion and constitutional reform. Dadabhai Naoroji, Gopal Krishna Gokhale, Pherozeshah Mehta and Surendranath Banerjea were important early figures.

Swadeshi and assertive nationalism

The 1905 partition of Bengal stimulated boycott, swadeshi, national education and wider mobilisation. Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal and Lala Lajpat Rai became prominent. The Surat split occurred in 1907.

Home Rule to Gandhian phase

Tilak and Annie Besant organised Home Rule Leagues in 1916. Gandhi's Champaran, Ahmedabad and Kheda interventions were followed by the Rowlatt agitation, Non-Cooperation, Civil Disobedience and Quit India, transforming Congress into a mass political organisation.

Parallel strands

Revolutionary groups, labour and peasant movements, socialist currents, constitutionalists, women's organisations, Subhas Bose and the INA, and movements in princely states all contributed to anti-colonial politics. Nationalism was socially and ideologically diverse.

Toward independence

World War II transformed the political context. The Cripps Mission failed to secure agreement in 1942. The Cabinet Mission arrived in 1946. Intensifying Congress–League disagreement, communal violence and British withdrawal culminated in Partition and independence in August 1947.

Andhra

Andhra nationalism included Swadeshi and Home Rule activity, Chirala-Perala, Alluri's Rampa rebellion, Gandhian mobilisation, Salt Satyagraha and extensive constructive work.
