Kakatiyas & Reddis

APPSC Group-I • Andhra Regional History • Reading-ready chapter

1. Kakatiya rise

The Kakatiyas developed from a regional power into a major Deccan polity centred on Warangal. Ganapatideva and Rudrama Devi are especially important; Prataparudra II was the last major ruler before Delhi Sultanate conquest.

2. Irrigation and agriculture

Kakatiya rulers and local elites supported tanks and irrigation works suited to the Deccan environment. Agrarian expansion strengthened the regional economy.

3. Administration and military structure

Nayaka-type military arrangements, forts and local elites were important to Kakatiya political organisation. Study them in the context of state formation rather than assuming a later identical institution.

4. Warangal and trade

Warangal was an important political and commercial centre. Motupalli is associated with maritime trade and commercial activity on the Andhra coast.

5. Rudrama Devi

Rudrama Devi is significant as a woman ruler in medieval Deccan history. Her reign should be studied through inscriptions, political alliances and military challenges.

6. Reddi kingdoms

After Kakatiya decline, Reddi powers emerged in coastal Andhra, with Kondaveedu and Rajahmundry among important centres. Their history connects regional politics, agriculture, literature and temple patronage.

7. Literature and culture

Telugu literary development continued through the medieval period, with regional courts supporting literary and cultural activity.

8. Mains

Explain Kakatiya legacy through political consolidation + irrigation + trade + architecture/culture, then show the transition to Reddi and later Vijayanagara politics.
