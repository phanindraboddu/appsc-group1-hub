Actual study notes — Kakatiyas & Reddis

Kakatiya rise

The Kakatiyas developed from regional chiefs into a major Telugu-speaking kingdom, with Orugallu (Warangal) as their celebrated capital. Important rulers include Prola II, Rudradeva, Ganapatideva, Rudramadevi and Prataparudra II.

Ganapatideva and Rudramadevi

Ganapatideva greatly expanded Kakatiya authority and strengthened links with the coast. Rudramadevi was one of the most notable women rulers in Indian history and faced challenges from powerful regional chiefs while maintaining the kingdom.

Prataparudra and Delhi

Under Prataparudra II, Kakatiya power confronted repeated incursions from the Delhi Sultanate. Malik Kafur's campaign reached Warangal; later Tughlaq forces captured the kingdom in the early fourteenth century.

Administration — Nayankara

Kakatiya rule relied significantly on nayakas and military-service relationships. For APPSC, compare but do not automatically equate Kakatiya nayankara arrangements with later Vijayanagara practice.

Irrigation and economy

  • Kakatiya rule is strongly associated with tank irrigation and agrarian expansion in Telangana.
  • Important tanks and local water structures supported settlement and cultivation.
  • Motupalli was a significant port linked with overseas commerce; inscriptions connected with the port illuminate trade policy.

Art and architecture

  • Thousand Pillar Temple at Hanamkonda is a major Kakatiya monument.
  • Ramappa (Rudreshwara) Temple at Palampet is another celebrated example.
  • Warangal fortifications and gateways are iconic features of Kakatiya material culture.

Reddi kingdoms

After Kakatiya decline, several regional powers emerged. The Reddis established important centres at Kondavidu and Rajahmundry. Prolaya Vema Reddi is associated with the foundation of Kondavidu Reddi power.

Literature and culture

The Reddi period contributed strongly to Telugu literary patronage. Srinatha is a major literary figure associated with the age. Reddi politics must be studied alongside Vijayanagara, Gajapati and other regional powers.
