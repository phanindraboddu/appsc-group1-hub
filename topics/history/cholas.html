Actual study notes — Cholas

Rise and rulers

The imperial Cholas emerged as a major south Indian power from the Kaveri delta. Vijayalaya founded the medieval Chola line. Rajaraja I and Rajendra I brought the empire to its greatest power.

Rajaraja I

  • Expanded Chola authority across south India and Sri Lanka.
  • Built the monumental Brihadisvara Temple at Thanjavur.
  • Strengthened administration and revenue organisation.

Rajendra I

  • Continued expansion and led the celebrated northern/Ganga campaign.
  • Founded Gangaikondacholapuram.
  • Chola naval expedition against Srivijaya demonstrates the Bay of Bengal dimension of Chola power.

Administration and local institutions

The empire was divided into territorial units while village institutions played important roles. Inscriptions from Uttaramerur are famous for information about local assemblies and committee selection. Do not generalise one locality's detailed arrangements to every village in the empire.

Economy and trade

The Kaveri delta supported intensive agriculture and irrigation. Merchant groups and ports connected south India with Sri Lanka and Southeast Asia. Temples acted as major religious, landed and economic institutions.

Art and culture

Chola temples represent a high point of Dravida architecture. Bronze sculpture, especially images of Nataraja, is internationally renowned. Tamil devotional and temple culture remained important.

Andhra/Vengi connection

Chola involvement in Eastern Chalukya succession politics tied Tamil and Telugu regions closely together. Matrimonial alliances helped create the Chalukya-Chola political synthesis; Kulottunga I is central to this connection.
