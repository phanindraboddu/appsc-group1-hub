Cholas

APPSC Group-I • South Indian History • Reading-ready chapter

1. Imperial Cholas

Vijayalaya established Chola power in the Kaveri region; Rajaraja I and Rajendra I transformed it into a major South Indian imperial power.

2. Administration

Chola inscriptions record territorial divisions, taxation, landholding and local institutions. Ur, sabha and nagaram are important terms, but local bodies should not be equated uncritically with modern democracy.

3. Irrigation and agriculture

The Kaveri delta supported intensive agriculture. Tanks, canals and water-management institutions were central to agrarian production.

4. Maritime activity

Chola power participated in Bay of Bengal trade and naval expeditions. Trade connected South India with Sri Lanka and Southeast Asian networks.

5. Temples

Temples functioned as religious, economic and cultural institutions. The Brihadisvara temple at Thanjavur is a major example of Chola architecture.

6. Literature and culture

Tamil devotional traditions, inscriptions, bronze sculpture and temple culture are major areas of study.

7. AP connection

Chola influence and contacts matter for the eastern coast and wider Bay of Bengal networks. Study Cholas alongside Eastern Chalukyas rather than as an isolated dynasty.

8. Mains

State formation → agrarian base → local institutions → temples → maritime trade → cultural legacy.
