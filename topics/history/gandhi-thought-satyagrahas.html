Actual study notes — Gandhi: Thought & Satyagrahas

Core ideas

Gandhi's politics joined satya (truth), ahimsa (non-violence) and satyagraha (insistence on truth through disciplined non-violent resistance). His thought also included swaraj, swadeshi, constructive work, communal harmony, removal of untouchability and an ethical critique of modern civilisation.

South Africa

Gandhi developed and tested satyagraha in South Africa before returning to India in 1915.

Early Indian satyagrahas

  • Champaran, 1917: indigo cultivators.
  • Ahmedabad, 1918: mill workers.
  • Kheda, 1918: revenue relief amid agrarian distress.

Rowlatt and Non-Cooperation

The Rowlatt agitation of 1919 marked an attempted all-India satyagraha. The Jallianwala Bagh massacre intensified opposition to colonial rule. Gandhi linked the Khilafat and nationalist causes in the Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–22), which he suspended after Chauri Chaura.

Civil Disobedience

The Salt March from Sabarmati to Dandi in 1930 challenged the salt laws and launched mass civil disobedience. The Gandhi-Irwin Pact (1931) temporarily suspended the movement and Gandhi attended the Second Round Table Conference.

Quit India

In August 1942 Congress launched Quit India. Gandhi's “Do or Die” call symbolised the demand for immediate British withdrawal. Because top leaders were arrested quickly, subsequent resistance was often locally organised.

Constructive programme and social questions

Gandhi promoted khadi, village industries, sanitation, basic education and campaigns against untouchability. His positions on caste and separate electorates generated major debates, including disagreements with B.R. Ambedkar. Present these differences through their documented ideas rather than reducing either thinker to slogans.

Andhra

Gandhian programmes strongly influenced Andhra nationalism through non-cooperation, khadi, constructive activity, salt satyagraha and local mass mobilisation.
