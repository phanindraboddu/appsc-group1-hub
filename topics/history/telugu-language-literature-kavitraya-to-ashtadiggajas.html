Telugu Language & Literature: Kavitraya to Ashtadiggajas

APPSC Group-I • Andhra-focused Prelims + Mains

Core coverage

Development of Telugu literary culture; Nannaya, Tikkana and Errana (Kavitrayam); later courtly and devotional traditions; Vijayanagara literary florescence and Ashtadiggajas.

Prelims focus

  • Chronology and sequence
  • Places, dynasties/leaders and institutions
  • Inscriptions, literary works and authors
  • Religion, art and architecture
  • Andhra-specific political, social and economic developments

Mains dimensions

Background → chronology → political/administrative developments → society & economy → culture/literature → Andhra significance → continuity/change → conclusion.

APPSC high-yield core

  • Kavitraya: Nannaya, Tikkana and Errana are associated with the Telugu rendering of the Mahabharata across different periods and political settings.
  • Nannaya: associated with Rajaraja Narendra and the Eastern Chalukya/Vengi cultural milieu; crucial to the early classical Telugu literary tradition.
  • Tikkana: linked with the Nellore Chola period; revise his contribution to continuation of the Mahabharata translation tradition.
  • Errana: completed the remaining portion in the Kavitraya tradition and is associated with the Reddi-period literary milieu.
  • Vijayanagara: Krishnadevaraya's reign is a major high point of Telugu court literature; he authored Amuktamalyada.
  • Ashtadiggajas: revise the major poets and works associated with the Vijayanagara literary tradition, while noting that lists/attributions can vary in later tradition.
  • Literature as history: connect literary growth with court patronage, temple culture, vernacularisation, religious traditions and regional identity.

Prelims focus

Poet–patron • poet–work • dynasty/period • Kavitraya sequence • Krishnadevaraya–Amuktamalyada • major prabandha traditions.

Mains angle

Organise chronologically: Eastern Chalukya beginnings → Kavitraya → Kakatiya/Reddi literary growth → Vijayanagara/prabandha age → language, patronage and socio-cultural significance.
