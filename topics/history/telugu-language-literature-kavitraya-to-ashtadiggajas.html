Actual study notes — Telugu Language & Literature: Kavitraya to Ashtadiggajas

Early Telugu

Telugu developed over centuries from spoken Dravidian roots into an inscriptional and literary language. Early inscriptions, including those associated with the Renati Cholas, are crucial evidence for its growing public use.

Kavitraya

The Telugu Mahabharata is associated with the celebrated Kavitraya: Nannaya, Tikkana and Errana. Their work spans different political periods and should not be presented as a single court project completed at one time.

  • Nannaya Bhattaraka — traditionally Adi Kavi; began the Mahabharata under Eastern Chalukya ruler Rajaraja Narendra.
  • Tikkana Somayaji — continued the epic in the later medieval period and is associated with the Nellore Chola milieu.
  • Errana (Yerrapragada) — completed the remaining portion and is associated with the Reddi age.

Kakatiya and Reddi literary culture

The Kakatiya period supported Telugu literary and inscriptional growth. Palakuriki Somanatha is important for Shaiva/Virashaiva literature. The Reddi period is strongly associated with Srinatha, a major poet whose career crossed regional courts.

Vijayanagara and Krishnadevaraya

Vijayanagara became a major centre of Telugu literary patronage. Emperor Krishnadevaraya wrote Amuktamalyada in Telugu. Tradition associates his court with the Ashtadiggajas.

Ashtadiggajas

Allasani Peddana, author of Manucharitramu, is traditionally honoured as Andhra Kavita Pitamaha. Other names traditionally included in the group include Nandi Timmana, Dhurjati, Madayyagari Mallana, Ayyalaraju Ramabhadrudu, Pingali Surana, Ramarajabhushanudu and Tenali Ramakrishna; lists can vary in later literary tradition, so questions should be read carefully.
