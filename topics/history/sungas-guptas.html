Sungas & Guptas

APPSC Group-I • Ancient India • Reading-ready chapter

1. Post-Mauryan setting

The Sunga period followed Mauryan decline in parts of north India. Regional powers developed across the subcontinent.

2. Sungas

Pushyamitra Sunga is traditionally associated with the establishment of Sunga rule. Buddhist sites and Brahmanical traditions both continued in the broader period; avoid simplistic claims of universal religious persecution.

3. Indo-Greek interactions

North-western political contacts continued, with Indo-Greek rulers interacting with Indian states. This period is important for coinage and cultural exchange.

4. Gupta rise

The Gupta dynasty rose in north India and under rulers such as Chandragupta I, Samudragupta and Chandragupta II developed a major imperial formation.

5. Samudragupta

The Allahabad pillar inscription is a key source for his campaigns and political relationships. Distinguish direct annexation from tribute/subordinate relationships described in the inscription.

6. Economy and society

Agrarian expansion, land grants, trade and urban change shaped the period. Social stratification and Brahmanical institutions became significant.

7. Science, literature and art

Sanskrit literature, mathematics/astronomy and sculpture flourished in the wider Gupta-age cultural environment. Avoid treating every achievement as created solely by the state.

8. Prelims/Mains

Revise inscriptions, rulers, political relationships, art, literature and economy as separate evidence-based themes.
