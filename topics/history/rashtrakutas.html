Rashtrakutas

APPSC Group-I • Prelims + Mains integrated

Core coverage

Manyakheta, Dantidurga, Krishna I, Amoghavarsha, tripartite struggle, trade, literature and Kailasa temple at Ellora.

Prelims checklist

Chronology • personalities/dynasties • places • texts/inscriptions • administration • economy • religion • art/architecture • source-based distinctions.

Mains framework

Context → political developments → administration → society/economy → religion/culture → art/literature → historical significance → Andhra/Deccan linkage where relevant.

Practice only — not PYQs.

Mains practice

Discuss the political, socio-economic and cultural significance of Rashtrakutas.

Revision rule

Remember 5 facts + 3 sources/places + 2 contributions + 1 analytical conclusion.

Exam-ready core

  • Deccan empire: the Rashtrakutas emerged as a major power after the Badami Chalukyas; Manyakheta became an important capital.
  • North Indian politics: they participated with the Palas and Gurjara-Pratiharas in the tripartite contest associated with Kanauj.
  • Major rulers: revise Dantidurga, Krishna I, Govinda III and Amoghavarsha I with their major political/cultural associations.
  • Architecture: the Kailasa temple at Ellora, associated with Krishna I, is a high-yield example of rock-cut architecture.
  • Literature: Sanskrit and Kannada traditions received patronage; Amoghavarsha is associated with literary activity.
  • Andhra connection: Rashtrakuta intervention in Vengi and relations with the Eastern Chalukyas are important for regional chronology.

Prelims focus

Tripartite struggle, Manyakheta, Ellora Kailasa, ruler–achievement pairs and Rashtrakuta–Eastern Chalukya relations.

Mains angle

Assess the Rashtrakutas through imperial reach, Deccan political networks, religious/cultural patronage, architecture and interaction with Vengi.
