Full chapter notes

Rise and rulers

The Rashtrakutas emerged as a major Deccan imperial power in the eighth century after displacing Chalukya authority. Dantidurga is associated with the rise of imperial power; Manyakheta later became their capital.

  • Krishna I: associated with the Kailasa temple at Ellora.
  • Dhruva and Govinda III: major northern campaigns and intervention in the struggle involving Kanauj.
  • Amoghavarsha I: long reign and major literary-cultural patronage; Kavirajamarga is associated with his age.

Kanauj and inter-regional politics

Rashtrakutas, Palas and Gurjara-Pratiharas competed for influence over Kanauj. Rashtrakuta campaigns demonstrate that Deccan and north Indian politics were closely connected.

Administration and economy

Kingship operated through provincial/local administration and subordinate chiefs. Agriculture, land revenue, grants and commercial networks sustained political and cultural activity.

Culture

  • Kailasa temple at Ellora is a landmark of monumental rock-cut architecture.
  • Patronage extended to Sanskrit, Kannada and Jain intellectual traditions.
  • Amoghavarsha's period is important in Kannada literary history.

Andhra linkage

Rashtrakutas repeatedly interacted and fought with the Eastern Chalukyas of Vengi, directly connecting Deccan imperial politics with Andhra history.
