Actual study notes — Satavahanas, Sangam, Sungas & Guptas

Post-Mauryan setting

The end of Mauryan imperial unity was followed by regional kingdoms and intensified commercial-cultural interaction. The Sungas ruled parts of north India; the Satavahanas became a major Deccan power; Tamilakam is illuminated by Sangam literature; the Guptas later built a major north Indian empire.

Sungas

Pushyamitra Sunga founded the dynasty after the Mauryas. Evidence for the period includes inscriptions, coins and monuments. Claims about systematic religious persecution require caution because sources are contested.

Satavahanas

Simuka is treated as an early founder. Gautamiputra Satakarni, praised in the Nasik inscription of Gautami Balashri, fought Western Kshatrapas. Satavahana rule connected the Deccan to inland and maritime trade. Prakrit inscriptions, coinage and Buddhist patronage are major themes; Amaravati is central for Andhra.

Sangam age

Sangam literature provides important evidence for early historic Tamilakam and the Chera, Chola and Pandya polities. Poems illuminate warfare, chiefs, bards, agriculture, exchange, ports and social life. Literary evidence must be combined with archaeology and inscriptions.

Guptas

Chandragupta I, Samudragupta and Chandragupta II were major rulers. The Allahabad/Prayaga pillar inscription composed by Harisena is a crucial source for Samudragupta. Gupta political authority combined imperial claims with varied relationships to subordinate and frontier rulers.

Culture and science

The Gupta age saw major developments in Sanskrit literature, temple architecture, sculpture, mathematics and astronomy. Kalidasa and Aryabhata are iconic names. Calling the entire period a universal “Golden Age” should be qualified because prosperity and social experience varied.
