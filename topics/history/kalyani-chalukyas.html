Full chapter notes

Rise and political history

The Western or Kalyani Chalukyas emerged in the late tenth century when Tailapa II overthrew Rashtrakuta authority. Their political centre later became associated with Kalyani (Basavakalyan).

  • Tailapa II: founder of the restored Western Chalukya power.
  • Someshvara I: an important ruler in the continuing struggle with the Cholas.
  • Vikramaditya VI: the most celebrated ruler; his accession is associated with the Chalukya-Vikrama era.

Chola rivalry and Vengi

The Western Chalukyas and Cholas competed for supremacy in the Deccan. Vengi was especially important because dynastic succession and strategic influence there repeatedly drew the Western Chalukyas, Eastern Chalukyas and Cholas into conflict. This is a major Andhra connection.

Administration and economy

The monarchy worked through territorial officials and powerful subordinate chiefs. Agriculture, irrigation, land grants, craft activity and temple-centred settlements supported the economy. Local institutions continued to matter alongside royal authority.

Art, architecture and literature

  • Western Chalukya architecture forms an important bridge between earlier Chalukya traditions and later Hoysala architecture.
  • Important centres include Lakkundi, Gadag, Dambal and Itagi.
  • The Mahadeva temple at Itagi is a notable example.
  • Vijnaneshwara, author of the Mitakshara commentary on Yajnavalkya Smriti, is associated with Vikramaditya VI's court.
  • Kannada and Sanskrit literary activity received patronage.
