Indus Valley Civilization (Harappan Civilization)

APPSC Group-I • Prelims facts + Mains analysis + revision

1. Chronology & discovery

The Harappan civilisation developed in the north-western subcontinent during the Bronze Age. For exam preparation, distinguish the Early, Mature and Late Harappan phases; the mature urban phase is conventionally placed around 2600–1900 BCE.

  • Harappa: on the Ravi; excavations associated with Daya Ram Sahni.
  • Mohenjo-daro: in Sindh; excavations associated with R. D. Banerji.
  • John Marshall: announced the discovery of the civilisation to the wider scholarly world in the 1920s.

3. Urban planning

  • Planned streets, baked-brick construction at many sites and sophisticated drainage are characteristic features.
  • Settlements often show differentiated sectors conventionally described as citadel/lower-town areas, though layouts varied.
  • Standardised weights and measures indicate organised craft and exchange systems.
  • Dholavira is especially important for reservoirs and water conservation.

4. Economy

Agriculture, animal husbandry, specialised crafts and internal/long-distance trade sustained Harappan settlements. Wheat and barley were important crops; cotton is particularly associated with the civilisation. Archaeological and Mesopotamian evidence indicates overseas contacts; Mesopotamian texts refer to a region called Meluhha, widely associated by scholars with the Indus sphere.

5. Society, religion & culture

  • The script remains undeciphered, so interpretations of political and religious organisation must be cautious.
  • Seals, figurines and ritual/bathing structures provide archaeological evidence, but labels such as “Mother Goddess” or “Proto-Shiva” are interpretations rather than deciphered textual facts.
  • No monumental royal palaces or royal tombs comparable to contemporary Egypt/Mesopotamia have been securely identified.

6. Art & technology

  • Bronze casting: famous dancing-girl figurine from Mohenjo-daro.
  • Stone sculpture: so-called “Priest-King” figure from Mohenjo-daro.
  • Steatite seals commonly depict animals and short inscriptions.
  • Bead-making, metallurgy, pottery and shell work demonstrate craft specialisation.

7. Decline / transformation

There is no single universally accepted cause. Explanations examine climatic/environmental change, changing river systems, declining long-distance trade and regional transformation. Avoid the old exam trap of presenting a single “Aryan invasion destroyed Harappa” explanation as established fact.

8. Prelims rapid revision

  • Great Bath → Mohenjo-daro
  • Major water-management system → Dholavira
  • Ploughed-field evidence → Kalibangan
  • Maritime/dockyard association → Lothal
  • Script → undeciphered
  • Urban phase → Bronze Age

9. Practice MCQs

  1. The Great Bath was found at: A) Harappa B) Mohenjo-daro C) Lothal D) Kalibangan
    Answer: B
  2. Dholavira is especially noted for: A) Ashokan edicts B) water management C) Gupta coins D) megaliths
    Answer: B
  3. Which statement is correct? A) Harappan script is fully deciphered B) Harappans knew urban drainage C) Iron was the defining Harappan metal D) Harappans left extensive deciphered literature
    Answer: B
  4. Kalibangan is associated with evidence of: A) ploughed field B) Great Bath C) rock-cut caves D) stupas
    Answer: A
  5. Meluhha occurs in sources connected with: A) Mesopotamia B) China C) Rome only D) Sangam literature
    Answer: A

10. Mains practice

Question: Explain the principal features of Harappan urbanism and assess what they reveal about its economy and society.

Answer structure: brief chronology → town planning/drainage → water management → craft specialisation → weights/trade → social organisation inferred cautiously from archaeology → limitations due to undeciphered script → conclusion on urban sophistication.
