Full chapter notes

Hoysalas: rise and rulers

The Hoysalas rose in Karnataka and developed from regional chiefs into an important south Indian power. Their early centre was Belur and the later capital was Dwarasamudra (Halebidu).

  • Vishnuvardhana: a major ruler associated with expansion and important temple patronage.
  • Ballala II: strengthened Hoysala power during the period of conflict among major Deccan and south Indian kingdoms.

Hoysala art and culture

  • Architecture is known for complex plans, profuse sculpture and highly finished stone workmanship.
  • Chennakeshava Temple, Belur and Hoysaleshwara Temple, Halebidu are major examples.
  • The Kesava temple at Somanathapura belongs to the mature Hoysala architectural tradition.
  • Shaiva, Vaishnava and Jain traditions all formed part of the wider religious landscape.

Seuna/Yadavas of Devagiri

The Seunas or Yadavas became a major Deccan kingdom with Devagiri as capital. Bhillama V is associated with their assertion of independent power, while Singhana became one of their strongest rulers.

  • Their position connected the western and northern Deccan with neighbouring powers.
  • Devagiri later faced the expansion of the Delhi Sultanate, beginning with Alauddin Khalji's famous raid before his accession as Sultan and followed by deeper intervention under the Khaljis.

Political setting and Andhra connection

Hoysalas and Yadavas should be studied alongside the Kakatiyas of Warangal, Western Chalukyas, Cholas and other Deccan powers. Competition among these regional kingdoms shaped the political environment surrounding medieval Andhra.
