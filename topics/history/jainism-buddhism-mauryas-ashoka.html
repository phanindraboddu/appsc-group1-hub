Jainism, Buddhism, Mauryas & Ashoka

APPSC Group-I • Prelims + Mains integrated History

Core coverage

Teachings and councils of Jainism/Buddhism; rise of Magadha; Mauryan state and administration; Ashoka's dhamma, inscriptions, art and historical significance.

Prelims high-yield

Chronology • rulers/leaders • places • texts/inscriptions • institutions • terminology • art/architecture • causes and consequences • Andhra links.

Mains framework

Background → chronology → political/administrative features → society/economy → religion/culture → significance → Andhra connection → balanced conclusion.

Practice

Prepare a timeline, five factual prelims cards and a 250-word answer explaining the historical significance of Jainism, Buddhism, Mauryas & Ashoka. Practice only; do not label a question PYQ unless verified from the official paper.

Rapid revision

5 dates/events → 5 personalities → 5 terms → 3 cultural contributions → 2 Andhra links → 1 mains thesis.

Integrated revision core

  • Place Jainism and Buddhism in the context of sixth-century BCE social-economic change, urbanisation and questioning of ritual hierarchy.
  • Jainism: Mahavira, triratna, five vows, anekantavada and monastic/lay traditions.
  • Buddhism: Four Noble Truths, Eightfold Path, dependent origination, sangha and spread through patronage/trade networks.
  • Mauryas: Chandragupta, Bindusara and Ashoka; use inscriptions, archaeology, Greek accounts and literary texts carefully as different source types.
  • Ashoka: inscriptions are the principal contemporary evidence for dhamma; distinguish dhamma from a simple synonym for Buddhism.
  • Andhra link: Buddhist expansion and later flourishing at Amaravati/Nagarjunakonda form an important regional continuation.

Prelims traps

Doctrine–religion • council–place/patron • edict type • inscription language/script • dhamma vs personal Buddhist affiliation.

Mains synthesis

Second urbanisation → heterodox traditions → Magadhan expansion → Mauryan empire → Ashokan governance/dhamma → diffusion of Buddhism → long-term Andhra significance.
