European Trading Companies in India

APPSC Group-I • Detailed Prelims + Mains chapter

Core syllabus

  • Portuguese arrival and Estado da India
  • Dutch East India Company
  • English East India Company
  • French Compagnie des Indes
  • Factories, forts and coastal trade
  • Carnatic rivalry and British-French competition
  • From commerce to territorial power

High-yield facts

  • Vasco da Gama reached Calicut in 1498 by the sea route around the Cape of Good Hope.
  • The Portuguese established an early maritime trading network and strongholds on the western coast.
  • The English East India Company was chartered in 1600.
  • Dutch, English and French companies competed for Asian trade; their priorities and territorial trajectories differed.
  • The shift from trading privileges to territorial revenue and political power accelerated in the eighteenth century.

Historical analysis

Read this topic through state formation → administration → economy → society → religion/culture → regional interaction → change over time. In Mains, avoid a ruler-by-ruler narrative unless the question asks for chronology.

Prelims traps

  • Match personalities with the correct dynasty and period.
  • Distinguish an administrative institution from a modern equivalent.
  • Do not turn a literary tradition into direct administrative evidence without qualification.
  • Separate political control from cultural influence.

Practice MCQs — not official PYQs

  1. Historical institutions should be interpreted in their period context. True
  2. A single ruler is always sufficient to explain the rise or fall of a dynasty. False
  3. Inscriptions, coins, architecture and literature can complement one another as historical sources. True
  4. These questions are official PYQs. False

Mains practice

Examine the political, economic and cultural significance of European Trading Companies in India. Use chronology only where it helps establish change and causation.

Structure: context → political development → administration/economy → society/culture → regional impact → conclusion.
