Full chapter notes

Why Europeans came by sea

European maritime expansion was driven by demand for Asian commodities, search for direct sea routes, advances in navigation and intense commercial competition. India's textiles, spices and other goods became central to European trading networks.

Portuguese

  • Vasco da Gama reached Calicut in 1498.
  • The Portuguese developed fortified maritime bases and sought to control important sea routes.
  • Goa, captured under Afonso de Albuquerque in 1510, became their principal Indian centre.
  • The cartaz system was connected with Portuguese attempts to regulate maritime trade.

Dutch

The Dutch East India Company (VOC) was founded in 1602. Dutch interests in India were strongly linked to textiles and the wider Indian Ocean trade, although their principal Asian focus increasingly centred on the Indonesian spice trade.

English East India Company

  • The English East India Company received its royal charter in 1600.
  • It gradually established trading settlements on India's coasts, including Surat, Madras, Bombay and Calcutta.
  • Commercial competition increasingly acquired a political and military dimension during the eighteenth century.

French

The French East India Company was founded in the seventeenth century; Pondicherry became a major French centre. Anglo-French rivalry in the Carnatic Wars became part of the wider struggle for political influence in India.

Why the English gained predominance

  • Naval and commercial resources, company finance and access to expanding trade networks.
  • Success against European rivals, particularly in the eighteenth-century Anglo-French struggle.
  • Ability to exploit changing regional political conditions and convert commercial bases into territorial power.
  • After Bengal victories and revenue acquisition, the Company's fiscal resources greatly strengthened its military-political capacity.

From trade to political power

Plassey (1757) and Buxar (1764) were crucial stages in Company expansion in Bengal. The grant of Diwani rights in Bengal, Bihar and Orissa in 1765 gave the Company a major revenue base. Do not describe European arrival itself as immediate colonial rule—the transformation took centuries.

Andhra coast connection

  • The Coromandel coast was important for textile trade and European factories.
  • Masulipatnam (Machilipatnam) was a major port and trading centre connected with several European companies.
  • Dutch activity at Pulicat and English/French competition in the wider Coromandel-Carnatic region are important for APPSC.
  • European commercial activity interacted with Golconda/Qutb Shahi authority and regional merchant networks before later Company political expansion.
