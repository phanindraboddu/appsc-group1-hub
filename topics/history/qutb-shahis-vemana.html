Actual study notes — Qutb Shahis & Vemana

Rise of Golconda

The Qutb Shahi state emerged from the fragmentation of the Bahmani Sultanate. Sultan Quli Qutb-ul-Mulk established an increasingly independent Golconda polity in the early sixteenth century.

Hyderabad

Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah founded Hyderabad in 1591; the Charminar became its iconic monument. Golconda and Hyderabad occupied strategic positions linking the Deccan interior with eastern commercial networks.

Economy

The kingdom benefited from agriculture, textile production, ports and the famous diamond trade associated with the Golconda region. Machilipatnam was a crucial Coromandel port through which Indian and foreign merchants participated in Indian Ocean trade.

Culture

Qutb Shahi courts patronised Persian, Dakhni and regional cultural traditions. Their political society involved Muslim and Hindu elites and Telugu-speaking populations; the period is therefore best understood through the composite cultural environment of the Deccan rather than a single communal lens.

End of the dynasty

Mughal emperor Aurangzeb conquered Golconda in 1687, ending Qutb Shahi sovereignty.

Vemana

Vemana occupies a major place in Telugu popular literary tradition. His concise moral and social verses, commonly called Vemana padyalu, criticise hypocrisy, empty ritual and social pretension while stressing ethical conduct and practical wisdom. Details of his exact date and biography are uncertain, so APPSC answers should distinguish the strong literary tradition from uncertain biographical claims.
