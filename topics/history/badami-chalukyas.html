Exam-ready core

  • Political centre: Vatapi (Badami) was the capital; Aihole and Pattadakal are essential cultural sites.
  • Pulakeshin II: the best-known ruler; revise his conflict with Harsha and relations with Pallavas. The Aihole inscription of Ravikirti is a key source.
  • Deccan politics: Chalukya-Pallava rivalry shaped the peninsula and linked western Deccan politics with eastern/southern regions.
  • Administration: monarchy worked through territorial divisions, feudatories and local institutions; inscriptions are important evidence.
  • Architecture: Badami cave temples, Aihole experiments and Pattadakal monuments illustrate the development and interaction of northern and southern temple forms.

Full chapter notes

Rise and rulers

The Early or Badami Chalukyas established a major Deccan kingdom in the sixth century, with Vatapi (Badami) as capital. Pulakeshin I strengthened the dynasty; Kirtivarman I expanded it; Mangalesha followed him. Pulakeshin II was its most celebrated ruler.

Pulakeshin II

  • The Aihole inscription composed by Ravikirti is a major source for his reign.
  • He checked Harshavardhana's southward expansion near the Narmada region.
  • He fought the Pallavas; the long Chalukya–Pallava rivalry shaped Deccan–Tamil politics.
  • Narasimhavarman I of the Pallavas later captured Vatapi, showing that the rivalry changed direction.

Administration & society

The monarchy worked through provincial/local units and subordinate chiefs. Agriculture and land revenue were central; grants to Brahmanas and religious establishments contributed to agrarian and cultural expansion.

Religion, art & architecture

  • Royal patronage encompassed Brahmanical traditions and Jainism.
  • Badami: rock-cut cave temples.
  • Aihole: major centre of experimentation in temple architecture.
  • Pattadakal: temples displaying northern and southern architectural idioms; Virupaksha temple is especially important.

Andhra linkage

Pulakeshin II's eastern expansion is linked with the establishment of the Eastern Chalukya line in Vengi under his brother Kubja Vishnuvardhana—an essential APPSC connection.
