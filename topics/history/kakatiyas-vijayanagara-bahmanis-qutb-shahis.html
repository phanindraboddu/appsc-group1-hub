Actual study notes — Kakatiyas, Vijayanagara, Bahmanis & Qutb Shahis

Kakatiyas

From Warangal, the Kakatiyas built a major Telugu kingdom. Ganapatideva expanded authority, Rudramadevi maintained the state amid elite challenges, and Prataparudra faced Delhi Sultanate incursions. Tank irrigation, nayaka relationships, Motupalli trade and temples such as Ramappa are major themes.

Vijayanagara

Founded conventionally in 1336 by Harihara and Bukka, Vijayanagara passed through Sangama, Saluva, Tuluva and Aravidu dynasties. Krishnadevaraya (1509–29) represents its political and cultural zenith; his Andhra campaigns, Raichur victory and Amuktamalyada are essential APPSC areas.

Bahmani Sultanate

The Bahmani Sultanate was established in 1347 and became Vijayanagara's principal Deccan rival. Conflict often focused on strategic territory and resources, especially the Raichur Doab. The state later fragmented into successor sultanates.

Qutb Shahis of Golconda

The Qutb Shahi dynasty emerged from the Bahmani successor-state system and ruled Golconda/Hyderabad. Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah founded Hyderabad in 1591 and is associated with the Charminar. The kingdom benefited from textile and diamond-linked commercial networks and patronised a composite Deccani cultural world.

Andhra/Telugu culture

These kingdoms shaped Telugu political and cultural history through irrigation, ports, temples, forts, literature and multilingual courts. Vijayanagara patronage of Telugu literature and Qutb Shahi engagement with Telugu-speaking society are especially important.
