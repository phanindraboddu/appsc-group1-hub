1857 & Socio-Religious Reform

APPSC Group-I • Prelims + Mains integrated History

Core coverage

Causes, centres, leaders, nature and consequences of the Revolt of 1857; nineteenth-century reform movements, organisations, debates and social impact.

Prelims high-yield

Chronology • rulers/leaders • places • texts/inscriptions • institutions • terminology • art/architecture • causes and consequences • Andhra links.

Mains framework

Background → chronology → political/administrative features → society/economy → religion/culture → significance → Andhra connection → balanced conclusion.

Practice

Prepare a timeline, five factual prelims cards and a 250-word answer explaining the historical significance of 1857 & Socio-Religious Reform. Practice only; do not label a question PYQ unless verified from the official paper.

Rapid revision

5 dates/events → 5 personalities → 5 terms → 3 cultural contributions → 2 Andhra links → 1 mains thesis.

Integrated revision core

  • 1857: political annexation, military grievances, economic pressures and socio-religious anxieties combined differently across regions.
  • Prepare Delhi, Kanpur, Lucknow, Jhansi and Bihar with leaders; also know why participation was geographically uneven.
  • Aftermath: Crown rule replaced Company rule in 1858 and colonial policy toward princes, army organisation and governance changed.
  • Reform movements: Brahmo Samaj, Arya Samaj, Prarthana Samaj, Ramakrishna Mission, Aligarh and other initiatives differed in theology, methods and social constituencies.
  • Andhra: Kandukuri Veeresalingam links social reform, women's education, widow remarriage, journalism and Telugu modernity.

Prelims focus

1857 centre–leader • 1858 change • reformer–organisation • organisation–objective • Andhra reformers.

Mains synthesis

Colonial transformation → resistance of 1857 → administrative aftermath → reform/public debate → education/print → emergence of new political consciousness.
