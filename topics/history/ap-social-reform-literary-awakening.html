Actual study notes — Andhra Social Reform & Literary Awakening

Background

Nineteenth- and early twentieth-century Andhra experienced expanding print culture, education, associations and debate over social practices. Social reform and literary modernisation became mutually reinforcing parts of a new public sphere.

Kandukuri Veeresalingam

Veeresalingam was a pioneering advocate of widow remarriage and women's education and a major figure in modern Telugu prose and journalism. His reform activity challenged entrenched social practices and helped create organised public debate.

Gurajada Apparao

Gurajada's Kanyasulkam became a landmark of modern Telugu drama and social criticism. His literary work is important for the movement toward accessible spoken-language expression and critique of social hypocrisy.

Gidugu Ramamurthy

Gidugu championed vyavaharika bhasha, or spoken/functional Telugu, against excessive dependence on highly classical literary forms. The language movement widened the debate over education and accessibility.

Other dimensions

Libraries, newspapers, journals and voluntary associations helped spread reform and nationalist ideas. Debates over caste, women's status, education and language were connected but not identical; different reformers adopted different positions.
