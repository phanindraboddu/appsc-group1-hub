Actual study notes — 19th-Century Reform Movements

Context

Colonial rule, print culture, new education, missionary criticism and indigenous intellectual debate produced multiple reform and revival movements. They differed sharply in theology, social base and methods; do not treat them as one organisation.

Brahmo Samaj

Raja Rammohan Roy campaigned against sati, defended women's rights and advocated modern education. He founded the Brahmo Sabha in 1828, later known as Brahmo Samaj. Debendranath Tagore and Keshab Chandra Sen shaped later phases.

Young Bengal & Prarthana Samaj

Henry Derozio influenced Young Bengal. Prarthana Samaj in western India advocated social reform; M.G. Ranade was a major associated figure.

Arya Samaj

Swami Dayanand Saraswati founded Arya Samaj in 1875 and called for a return to Vedic authority. Its educational and shuddhi activities became influential.

Ramakrishna–Vivekananda

Swami Vivekananda, disciple of Ramakrishna, founded the Ramakrishna Mission in 1897, combining spiritual teaching with organised social service.

Anti-caste and women's reform

Jyotirao and Savitribai Phule challenged caste oppression and promoted education, especially for women and marginalised groups. The Satyashodhak Samaj was founded in 1873. Sir Syed Ahmad Khan led the Aligarh movement for modern education among Muslims. Reform debates also concerned widow remarriage, child marriage and women's education.

Andhra connection

Kandukuri Veeresalingam was central to widow remarriage, women's education, social reform and modern Telugu public life. APPSC answers should connect all-India reform with Andhra's literary and social awakening.
