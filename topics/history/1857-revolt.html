Revolt of 1857

APPSC Group-I • Modern India • Deep reading chapter

Background

The revolt emerged from accumulated military, political, economic and social grievances. The cartridge issue acted as an immediate trigger in the sepoy uprising, but it does not explain the entire revolt.

Political causes

Annexations and the displacement of ruling houses created political resentment in several regions. Doctrine of Lapse is important but was not the sole cause.

Economic & social causes

Revenue pressures, changes in landed relations, loss of privileges and anxieties over social/religious intervention contributed in different areas.

Major centres & leaders

Delhi, Kanpur, Lucknow, Jhansi and Bihar were major centres. Bahadur Shah II, Nana Sahib, Rani Lakshmibai, Begum Hazrat Mahal and Kunwar Singh are high-yield associations.

Nature & limitations

Participation varied greatly by region, class and community; the revolt did not become an all-India unified movement.

Consequences

The East India Company's rule ended and the British Crown assumed direct rule in 1858. Administrative, military and political policies changed thereafter.

APPSC angle

Study the extent and character of 1857 in the wider context of southern India, where the pattern differed from the main north Indian centres.

Mains

Discuss 1857 as a multi-causal revolt, then assess its regional variation, limitations and long-term consequences.
