Actual study notes — Sardar Patel & Subhas Chandra Bose

Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel

Patel emerged from Gandhian mass politics and became a major Congress organiser. The Bardoli Satyagraha (1928) against enhanced land revenue is closely associated with his leadership and the title “Sardar”.

Integration after independence

As Deputy Prime Minister and Home Minister, Patel, working with V.P. Menon and others, played a central role in integrating princely states into the Indian Union through instruments of accession, negotiation and, in some cases, coercive state action. Integration was a collective governmental process; avoid attributing every decision to one individual alone.

Subhas Chandra Bose

Bose represented a more militant anti-colonial strategy while sharing the goal of independence. He was elected Congress president at Haripura (1938) and again at Tripuri (1939), after which political differences led to his resignation and formation of the Forward Bloc.

INA and Azad Hind

During World War II, Bose reached Southeast Asia and assumed leadership of the Indian National Army movement. The Provisional Government of Azad Hind was proclaimed in 1943. The INA fought alongside Japanese forces in the Burma–India theatre. Its military campaign failed, but the subsequent INA trials became politically significant in late-colonial India.
