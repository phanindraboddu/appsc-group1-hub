Actual study notes — Prehistoric & Ancient India

Prehistory: how we know

Prehistory is reconstructed mainly from archaeology because written records are absent. Stone tools, habitation sites, burials, pottery, plant and animal remains help trace changes in subsistence and settlement.

Palaeolithic

Hunter-gatherer communities used chipped stone tools. The Indian Palaeolithic is commonly discussed as Lower, Middle and Upper phases, but dates vary regionally. Bhimbetka is important for long human occupation and later rock art.

Mesolithic

Microliths are a characteristic tool type. Hunting, gathering and fishing continued while some sites show changing settlement and subsistence patterns.

Neolithic

Food production, domestication, more settled village life and polished stone tools became important. Neolithic developments occurred at different times in different regions; there was no single uniform “Neolithic revolution” across India.

Chalcolithic and Megalithic cultures

Chalcolithic communities used copper alongside stone and developed regional farming cultures. In peninsular India, Iron Age–Megalithic traditions are especially important; burials, iron objects and distinctive ceramics provide evidence for social and technological change.

Harappan civilisation

The Mature Harappan phase (c. 2600–1900 BCE) featured planned settlements, drainage, craft specialisation, standardised weights, long-distance trade and an undeciphered script. Major sites include Harappa, Mohenjo-daro, Dholavira, Lothal, Kalibangan and Rakhigarhi. Political structure and religion remain subjects of interpretation because the script is undeciphered.

Vedic to territorial states

Rig Vedic society differed from Later Vedic society in settlement, political organisation, economy and ritual. By about the sixth century BCE, sixteen Mahajanapadas are known from textual traditions, and Magadha emerged as the strongest state.

Andhra perspective

APPSC preparation should connect pan-Indian prehistory with archaeological cultures of the Deccan and Andhra, especially Neolithic/Chalcolithic and Iron Age–Megalithic evidence, without forcing northern chronological models unchanged onto the south.
