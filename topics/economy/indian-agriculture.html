1. Importance of Indian Agriculture

Agriculture continues to play a fundamental role in India's economy even though its relative contribution to national income has declined with structural transformation.

Its importance extends beyond crop production because agriculture and allied activities support employment, rural incomes, food security, industrial raw materials and domestic demand.

Large workforce dependence + relatively smaller share of national income → Lower income/productivity per agricultural worker
✍️ MAINS INSIGHT

A declining share of agriculture in GDP does not mean agriculture has become unimportant. The key structural issue is the continued dependence of a large workforce on a sector producing a comparatively smaller share of national output.

2. Agricultural Seasons

Feature Kharif Rabi Zaid
General sowing period Monsoon onset Autumn/Winter Summer
General harvesting period Autumn Spring/Early Summer Before/around monsoon
Examples Rice, maize, cotton, groundnut Wheat, gram, mustard Watermelon, cucumber, vegetables
🎯 PRELIMS TRAP

A crop should not always be treated as belonging exclusively to one season throughout India. Cropping seasons can vary according to climate, irrigation and regional agricultural practices.

3. Classification of Crops

Cereals: Rice, wheat, maize, jowar, bajra and ragi.

Pulses: Gram, tur/arhar, urad, moong and masur.

Commercial crops: Cotton, sugarcane, tobacco, jute and oilseeds.

Plantation crops: Tea, coffee and rubber.

Horticulture: Fruits, vegetables, spices, flowers and related high-value crops.

4. Cropping Pattern

Cropping pattern refers to the distribution or proportion of cultivated area devoted to different crops during a particular period.

A change from paddy cultivation towards horticulture, pulses or other crops therefore represents a change in cropping pattern.

Major determinants

Nature + Water + Soil + Market + Prices + Technology

Other influences include farm size, availability of agricultural credit, government procurement, dietary demand, infrastructure and government policy.

5. Cropping Systems

System Meaning Main idea
Monocropping Repeated cultivation of the same crop Specialisation
Multiple Cropping More than one crop on the same land during an agricultural year Greater land-use intensity
Mixed Cropping Two or more crops simultaneously without definite row arrangement Risk reduction
Intercropping Two or more crops simultaneously in a definite row arrangement Efficient resource utilisation
Crop Rotation Different crops grown sequentially according to a planned cycle Soil and crop management
🔥 HIGH-YIELD DIFFERENCE

Mixed Cropping: No definite row arrangement.

Intercropping: Definite row arrangement.

6. Structural Problems of Indian Agriculture

LAND → WATER → INPUTS → CREDIT → MARKET → PRICE → CLIMATE

Land: Small and fragmented holdings can restrict economies of scale.

Water: Uneven irrigation availability and rainfall dependence.

Inputs: Cost, quality and access issues involving seeds, fertilisers, pesticides and machinery.

Credit: Unequal access to affordable institutional finance.

Markets: Storage, cold-chain, transport and marketing constraints.

Prices: Price volatility and uneven access to procurement mechanisms.

Climate: Droughts, floods, cyclones, heat stress and rainfall variability.

✍️ MAINS LINE TO REMEMBER

India's agricultural challenge is not merely to increase production, but to improve productivity, profitability, resilience and farmers' incomes.

7. Production vs Productivity

Production Productivity
Total quantity produced Output per unit of land/input
Example: 300 tonnes Example: 3 tonnes/hectare
Production = Area × Yield

Therefore, agricultural production can rise because cultivated area increases, productivity increases, or both.

8. Green Revolution

The Green Revolution was introduced in the context of India's food shortages and aimed at substantially increasing agricultural production and productivity.

HYV Seeds + Irrigation + Fertilisers + Pesticides + Credit + Mechanisation + MSP/Procurement

Major achievements

Higher foodgrain production, improved food self-sufficiency, higher productivity in major beneficiary regions and reduced dependence on food imports.

Major concerns

Regional inequality, concentration on selected crops, groundwater depletion, intensive chemical-input use, soil-related problems and unequal access to the benefits of the new technology.

⚠️ APPSC TRAP

The Green Revolution was not simply the introduction of HYV seeds. It involved a package of technological, financial, irrigation, price-support and institutional measures.

9. Agricultural Diversification

Crops → Horticulture → Livestock → Dairy → Fisheries

Agricultural diversification can reduce excessive dependence on a limited number of crops and create opportunities for higher-value production and rural income generation.

Allied sectors are therefore increasingly important in discussions about agricultural growth and farmers' incomes.

✍️ Mains Corner

Probable Question

"Despite significant progress in foodgrain production, Indian agriculture continues to face structural challenges. Discuss."

Answer Framework

Introduction: Briefly establish India's progress in agricultural production and food security.

Body 1 — Achievements: Green Revolution, foodgrain production, irrigation, technology, diversification and institutional support.

Body 2 — Structural problems: Small holdings, irrigation gaps, input costs, credit, markets, price volatility, low productivity and climate vulnerability.

Body 3 — Way Forward: Water-use efficiency, diversification, better markets, technology, FPOs, agricultural infrastructure, climate-resilient agriculture and stronger rural non-farm opportunities.

Conclusion: Agricultural policy should move beyond production alone towards productivity, profitability, sustainability and resilience.

🧠 APPSC Prelims Practice

Q1. Which best describes cropping pattern?

A. Total agricultural production
B. Distribution of cultivated area among different crops
C. Productivity per hectare
D. Number of farmers cultivating a crop

Show Answer Answer: B

Cropping pattern concerns the distribution/proportion of cultivated area among different crops.

Q2. Intercropping differs from mixed cropping mainly because:

A. Only food crops are grown
B. Crops are grown in a definite row arrangement
C. Only two crops can be grown
D. Irrigation is compulsory

Show Answer Answer: B

Intercropping involves a definite spatial/row arrangement, unlike conventional mixed cropping.

Q3. Which was an important component of the Green Revolution?

A. Organic farming alone
B. HYV seeds combined with irrigation and modern inputs
C. Elimination of foodgrain cultivation
D. Removal of agricultural price support

Show Answer Answer: B

The Green Revolution relied on a package involving HYV seeds, irrigation, fertilisers and complementary institutional support.

Q4. Which is NOT the same as total agricultural production?

A. Total output
B. Aggregate quantity produced
C. Yield/productivity
D. Overall crop output

Show Answer Answer: C

Productivity refers to output per unit of land/input rather than total output.
