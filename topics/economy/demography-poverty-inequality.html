Demography, Poverty & Inequality

APPSC Group-I 2026 • Integrated Prelims + Mains preparation

1. Demography and development

Population affects labour supply, demand, savings, public services and dependency. A demographic dividend is a potential growth opportunity when the working-age share rises, but it requires health, education, skills and productive employment.

2. High-yield demographic concepts

ConceptMeaning
Dependency ratioRelates dependent-age population to working-age population under the definition used
Demographic dividendPotential economic benefit from favourable age structure, conditional on capabilities and jobs
FertilityChildbearing performance/rate measures; distinguish from population growth
MigrationMovement of people that affects labour markets, urbanisation and regional development
UrbanisationIncreasing share/concentration of population in urban areas

3. Poverty

Poverty can be studied through income/consumption thresholds as well as multidimensional deprivation. For Mains, distinguish absolute poverty from relative poverty, and monetary poverty from multidimensional poverty.

Do not mix poverty estimates from different methodologies or survey periods without explaining the basis.

4. Inequality

  • Income inequality: differences in flows of income.
  • Wealth inequality: differences in accumulated assets/net wealth.
  • Horizontal inequalities: disparities across social, gender, regional or other groups.
  • Equality of opportunity: access to education, health, finance, markets and institutions matters alongside final income distribution.

5. Poverty–inequality–growth linkage

Growth can reduce poverty when it creates productive employment and raises real incomes, but the magnitude depends on distribution, inflation, access to assets/services and the sectoral pattern of growth. Public policy therefore combines growth with social protection, human-capital investment and opportunity-enhancing interventions.

6. Andhra Pradesh lens

For APPSC Mains, examine district/regional disparities, rural livelihoods, agriculture, urbanisation, migration, education, health, social protection and employment. Current numerical claims should come from the latest official AP and Government of India statistical publications.

7. Practice MCQs

  1. A demographic dividend is: A) automatic cash payment B) potential benefit from a favourable age structure C) tax on population D) fall in exports
    Answer: B.
  2. Which directly measures distribution rather than average income? A) Per-capita income alone B) Gini-type inequality measure C) GDP deflator D) repo rate
    Answer: B.
  3. Multidimensional poverty differs from monetary poverty because it: A) ignores deprivation B) can incorporate several non-income deprivations C) measures only inflation D) measures exports
    Answer: B.
  4. A demographic dividend requires productive employment and human capital to be realised. A) True B) False
    Answer: A.
  5. Wealth is best understood as: A) a stock of accumulated assets/net wealth B) annual GDP only C) tax rate D) inflation
    Answer: A.

Practice MCQs — not APPSC PYQs.

8. Mains framework

Population structure → demographic opportunity → poverty concepts/trends → inequality dimensions → jobs/human capital/social protection → AP-specific disparities → policy convergence → conclusion.

9. Quick revision

Demographic dividend is potential, not automatic. Poverty ≠ inequality. Income ≠ wealth. Average income ≠ distribution.
